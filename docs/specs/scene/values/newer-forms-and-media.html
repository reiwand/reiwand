Newer forms#

Newer values add these forms, each with its feature:

Form Example Feature
Keyframe handles [2, 1, { "in": [0.5, 0], "out": [0.5, 0.2] }]: Bézier handles in time and value around a key of a number, the time of each within the neighbouring segment; out: [dt, dv] is the point (t + dt, v + dv) and in: [dt, dv] the point (t − dt, v − dv), so equal handles make a smooth key and dv: 0 a flat one; a side without a handle points a third of the way to the next key. Keys of positions, sizes, colors and other lists of numbers take handles too: there dv is a share of the way between the two keys (0 at the first, 1 at the second), so the curve shapes how fast every part moves at once and the value between is the usual blend of the two keys at that share; when both keys of a segment of such a field are written as one number (a pair or triple taken from one number, such as a scale of 2), dv is in value units as for a number, every part follows that one curve and the key is written back as the number it was; a side may also be a list of [dt, dv], one for each part ("out": [[0.5, 0], [0.2, 30]] for the x and the y of a position, up to four for the red, green, blue and alpha of a color, which then mix channel by channel instead of in OKLab), each in the units of its own part like the handle of a number, so the tracks of a value are shaped independently and even a part that does not change between the two keys can bend; a part missing from the list points a third of the way, and when one side of a segment is a list and the other a single pair, that pair is read as the same share of the way for every part; texts and switches take no handles tangents
Parts on their own { "x": { "keys": [[0, 0], [2, 100]] }, "y": { "cursor": "y", "scale": 100 } } for a position, r, g, b, a for a color; each part is a value of its own and a missing part is that of base, or 0 (alpha 1) tracks
Relative { "keys": [[0, 0], [1, 30]], "base": 120 } adds the animation to base (numbers, positions and colors, part by part; a lone number in an effect or script parameter is added to both parts of a pair and to the red, green and blue of a color, and the alpha of a sum stays within 0 to 1) relative_values
Time as a number { "clock": "day" } from 0 at midnight to almost 1 before the next; also hour (0 to 23), minute and second (0 to 59), weekday (1 for Monday to 7 for Sunday) and month (1 to 12), all in local time; a value that follows day changes at most once a minute, or every clock.step seconds (1 to 3600, always written; 60 is the usual value), and the others when they change clock_values
Dates { "date": "EEEE d 'de' MMMM yyyy" } (text only) with yyyy, yy, MMMM, MMM, MM, M, dd, d, EEEE, EEE, D (day of the year) and w (ISO week) besides the tokens of a clock, names in the language of the package (English or Spanish, English for the others); text between single quotes stays as written and '' writes a quote; a text without hours, minutes or seconds changes at midnight. A clock text keeps only the tokens of the time, and every other letter stays as written clock_dates
Frequency ranges { "audio": [0, 3], "kind": "relative", "unit": "db", "of": 16, "reduce": "max" } follows bands 0 to 3 of 16 (of is 16, 32 or 64, always written with an indexed band or a range; 64 is the number the editor suggests) and takes their mean (prefilled) or max audio_ranges
Shaping bounds: [a, b] maps a value to (x − a) / (b − a) clamped to 0 to 1, exponent (0.1 to 10) raises it, both before scale and offset; smooth: { "up", "down" } rises and falls at different speeds shaping
Layer as texture { "layer": "<id>" } for a texture or mask parameter of an effect: the layer is drawn on its own (even with visible: false) and fed to the effect aligned with the screen, so that each point of the effect reads the point of that layer above it, at the size the effect's layer has on the screen; it is drawn again only when that layer changes, and each one costs as much as a layer drawn on its own; layers may not feed themselves through other layers, masks or groups layer_textures

Events and triggers#

Timelines may name events, at most 64 { "t", "name" } with t within the duration and names of [a-z0-9_]{1,32}; each fires once per pass, also when a frame skips over it (on a pingpong timeline once each way), in the order of their time. A trigger or a play_on of event:<name> starts on it, as if it had started at the very moment of the event even when the frame came later, so its own events up to that frame, those at 0 included, fire in the same frame, and scripts receive it (timeline_events, which every platform runs). A still scene wakes for the next event only when a trigger, a sound or a script that the platform runs listens to its name. Clips of meshes and models name events the same way.

Triggers may also be click:<layer>, enter:<layer> and leave:<layer> for a layer that is solid or has hit bones, evaluated without scripts (layer_events; click: needs the clicks input, the others pointer): a click is a press and a release of any button both on the layer, which keeps the pointer from the press until the release (so dragging off it and letting go is no click), and enter/leave follow the topmost solid layer under the pointer. They also start sounds whose play_on names them. Pointer events never make extra frames: they are worked out when the scene draws a frame for the pointer it already follows, and a still pointer makes none. And media:play (the media player starts playing), media:pause (it pauses or stops, or the player goes away while it played), media:track (a new track, also the first one the scene sees) or media:cover (a new cover) (media_info, input media), evaluated without scripts when the reading of the player changes, never while the package lacks the grant or no player is there.

Media values#

media values follow what the media player of the computer plays, only while the scene is shown, the package declares media and the user allows it: the texts title, artist, album, album_artist, subtitle, genres and kind; the numbers position, duration (seconds) and progress (0 to 1); playing as a flag or number; and the colors primary, secondary and tertiary of the cover, text (readable over the primary color) and contrast (black or white). Texts show their placeholder while nothing plays or the grant is missing, and are empty without one: a text, or a map from language to text picked like the texts of the manifest by the language of the screen, at most 512 characters each, only beside a media text value (media_info); colors are those of the placeholder of the cover. The numbers also fit texts, written m:ss (h:mm:ss from an hour) for position and duration and as a whole percentage for progress. The position runs on from the last reading of the player at its rate, and starts again from where the player says it jumped (Seeked), without asking the player while it plays: a number that follows position or progress asks for at most 30 frames a second and a text one frame a second, only while the track plays and the scene is shown; paused, stopped or at the end of the track they wake nothing. An asset with media: current shows the cover of the track and one with media: previous the cover of the track before it (both still pictures, usable wherever a picture is: image layers, masks, effects, particles); path is the placeholder shown until a cover arrives, while nothing plays, without the grant and when the cover cannot be read. Covers are read once per track (the last eight fetched from the internet are kept in memory by address while media.remote_covers stays on, so they are not fetched again), also when a new track keeps the address of the one before (a cover with the same bytes stays the same cover), from file:// addresses of at most 16 MiB or, only with media.remote_covers on (dbus.md), from http and https addresses of at most 4 MiB within 5 seconds, pictures of more than 4096 pixels a side are refused, and JPEG covers are decoded already scaled down by the decoder (at most 1/8 of each side, never below 256 pixels on the long side) instead of at full size; they are reduced in memory to at most 256 pixels on the longer side in sRGB, keeping their shape, and never written to disk. A layer that shows a cover keeps its size, so a square size suits most covers. The colors are worked out once per cover: the three most common colors of the cover reduced to 64 × 64 (k-means of three in OKLab, transparent pixels left out), the most common first; text is the secondary or tertiary color with a WCAG contrast of at least 4.5 against the primary one, the higher one first, and otherwise white or black; contrast is white or black, whichever contrasts more with the primary color. Without a cover they are the colors of the placeholder of the first current cover asset, or dark greys with white text when there is none; smooth eases between them part by part.
