On this page: particle shaders, keeping distance, flocks, colliders, ropes, long trails, limits and instances.

Shaders, distance, flocks and colliders#

  • Particle shaders. render.shader names a package effect with stage: "particle", checked and limited like every package shader. Its fn particle(p: Sprite) -> Sprite runs for each corner of each sprite with the center, velocity, corner, uv, color, size, rotation (degrees), age and life, and may change all of them; the engine then draws the corner. It may read frame and the noise and color helpers. A shader that cannot be built is left out like any other package effect and the particles are drawn without it. Its module and pipelines are built away from drawing within the build time of package shaders, its time on the GPU counts like the time of a package effect, and one that takes too long to build, stopped the graphics card or is too slow is left out for the run the same way. Ropes and trails are drawn as usual. The WGSL may also define fn pixel(p: Pixel) -> vec4<f32>, which runs for each pixel of a sprite before the alpha. Pixel is sample: vec4<f32> (the texture, or the soft disc, after cutout), color: vec4<f32> (the color and alpha of the particle after color_over_life, the layer tint and the opacity, without the brightness and not multiplied by its alpha), brightness: f32 (the layer brightness times render.brightness), alpha: f32 (sample.a * color.a), uv: vec2<f32>, age: f32 and life: f32. It returns the straight color and alpha; the engine multiplies the color by that alpha and blends as always. Without fn pixel the result is sample * color, with the brightness already in the color, and every pixel is as before. With it, the brightness is not multiplied into the color of the sprite (a fn particle sees color without it), so the shader chooses where to apply it, and it clips before the alpha: fn pixel(p: Pixel) -> vec4<f32> { let lit = min(p.sample.rgb * p.color.rgb * p.brightness, vec3<f32>(1.0)); return vec4<f32>(lit, p.sample.a * p.color.a); }. A white sprite at alpha 0.5 and brightness 2.5 then stays at 127 over black, and an additive halo reaches the same level in all three channels. The function is checked and limited with the package shader (its signature, its work and its build time), may use the same helpers and frame, and reads no textures. If it does not compile the whole effect is left out. Every system that names the effect uses it; lit lights the result of fn pixel, refract only reads its alpha, and trails, long trails and ropes do not call it: they take the brightness as before, unclipped.
  • Keeping distance. keep_distance pulls each particle toward its target or pushes it away, as a spring that rests distance pixels from it: the acceleration is strength · (d − distance) along the line to the target, in pixels a second squared for each pixel. keep_between pulls each particle toward the nearest point of the segment from from to to by strength times its distance from it. Neither damps the motion, so a drag settles particles on the ring or the line.
  • Remap operators. A remap force applies the mapping of init.remap every 1/60 s step, with the age of the particle as one more input and random fixed for each particle and force. set replaces the value, add and sub add or subtract the mapped value each second and mul multiplies it by the mapped value each second (v · y^dt). The window of the force fades it in and out: set then moves the value only that fraction of the way.
  • Flocks. boids steers each particle with its neighbors closer than radius: separation pushes it away from them (their mean direction away, fading to nothing at radius, times radius), alignment turns its velocity toward their mean velocity and cohesion pulls it toward their mean position, each in pixels a second squared; max_speed (0 for none) caps its speed. Neighbors are found in a hash grid of cells radius pixels wide rebuilt every step (in compute shaders on the GPU, on the CPU in the order of the particles; the GPU fills a cell in any order, so where a cell holds more neighbors than count the two paths may pick different ones), and at most 32 of them count at quality 3 and 2, 16 at quality 1 and 8 at quality 0, or 8 and 16 on the two lower device tiers; a particle looks at no more than four times that many. Neighbors at the same place count too and push each pair apart along a direction picked for the pair, so a flock born at one point spreads out. The first boids force of a system sets the flock, which holds at most 8 192 particles, and 2 000 on the CPU.
  • Colliders. box keeps particles out of the box, or inside it with inside; it turns and scales with the layer. segment stops particles crossing the line from from to to from either side; particles that go around its ends pass. circle with inside keeps particles within the circle. layer keeps particles out of the opaque part (alpha above 0.5) of a layer that does not hold the system: the engine draws the layer as for emission, turns it into a field of at most 128 × 128 distances, rebuilt only when the drawing of the layer changes (at most four times a second), and the field follows the position, turn and scale of the layer between readings; while it was never drawn nothing collides. stick stops a particle where it touches and keeps it there until it dies, without forces or other colliders. kill ends the particle where it touches, which starts the death children of the system like any other death.

Ropes, long trails and limits#

  • Ropes. render.rope joins the living particles of a system's own emitters into one strip in the order they were born, from the newest, with subdivide (0 to 7) points between two particles on a Catmull-Rom curve (smooth, prefilled) or a straight line. It is width pixels wide, times size_over_life and the size an oscillate force gives, and takes the color of the particles with color_over_life. With a texture, the strip shows it along its length: uv_scale times per particle, moving uv_scroll times a second. The strip is built by a compute shader after the last step of a frame (and the same way on the CPU), with at most 4 096 segments (1 024 and 2 048 on the two lower device tiers) and fewer points between particles at quality 1 (half) and 0 (none). Ropes are not drawn for systems born from other particles. One particle emitted per step (max_per_step: 1) keeps the rope even.
  • Long trails. A trail longer than 16 steps, or with segments, subdivide, uv_scale, uv_scroll or textured, keeps segments + 1 positions (length without segments), one every (length − 1) / segments steps, so it covers length steps with fewer points, and draws a smooth ribbon through them with subdivide (0 to 7) points between two kept positions. It keeps at most 64 positions, 32 or 16 on the middle device tiers and none on the lowest, spacing them further apart when it keeps fewer; quality 2, 1 and 0 draw half, a quarter or none of them, and quality 1 and 0 fewer points between them. With textured and a texture, the ribbon shows it along its length, uv_scale times from head to tail, moving uv_scroll times a second.
  • Limits. A scene simulates at most 64 systems, counting children and layers with particles of their own, and their capacities share the 200 000 particles. prewarm runs in steps of 1/15 s, at most 30 million particle steps for the whole scene, and stops once it takes more than 200 ms. On the processor, when a frame has more than one step to take for some systems (a seek, or a jump of the clock), those systems share the 200 ms evenly, in their order, each passing on what it leaves to the next, so a later system is not left without particles; a frame of one step per system keeps the whole 200 ms for each, as before. A child never starts more particles in a step than it has room for.

Instances of a particles layer#

A particles layer may also set instance (alpha, size, count, speed, life, time_scale, brightness, color and points, which may be bound and multiply or move what the system does for that layer only, unless the system locks them; count, 0 to 4, multiplies the births and the room of the system, and above 1 the layer reserves that many times its capacity when the scene loads: the literal value, the upper end of the clamp of a binding, or 4 for a binding without one, so a count bound to a property should clamp it) and a render of its own that replaces the one of the system (particles2). Each end of the init ranges of numbers (life, speed, angle, size, rotation, spin, frame, alpha, radial, depth, depth_speed) and the numbers of emitter shapes (radius and inner of circle, threshold of layer, arc, radius and speed of around, span of between) may be bound like any value, for instance "size": [2, { "audio": "bass", "kind": "relative", "unit": "db", "scale": 8 }] (particle_values), and are read every frame and kept within the limits of the field; colors, velocities and the threshold of mask, read when the scene loads, stay literal. Births may also follow the sound or the pointer through the rate of the emitter, the instance multipliers of the layer or control points.
