On this page: moving the level while playing, the level the user chooses and the hard budget.

Moving the quality level while playing#

While a scene plays, the GPU time of its frames (measured as described below) moves the quality level. The desktop daemon and the phone steer by measured time alone, with the same controller (Pilot in crates/reiwand-scene/src/pilot.rs): its ceiling is q3 (one step under the tier level with power saving, lower while warm or hot, and never above the cap of the package), and the level the cost model fits is only where it starts, before any frame is measured. On the desktop the level never rises on battery, with power saving or while the GPU or CPU is warm, and the reason stays the one of the start (battery, saver or heat) while the level holds there; the phone also rises on battery. It keeps the GPU times of the last 32 frames and, from the 12th on, averages those between half and twice their median, so a few spikes do not count, against the frame interval of the rate it plays at (the user's cap or the display rate). When the average stays over 85 % of the interval for 2 seconds the level goes one step down; when it stays under half of the interval for 6 seconds (once the level above was measured: when the average times the ratio measured there fits three quarters of the interval), one step up, unless the power source, power saving or heat hold it. Two changes are at least 5 seconds apart, the window starts again after each, and a step up that comes back down within 30 seconds doubles the wait for the next one, up to four times; one that holds for 30 seconds halves it again, so a level measured too dear is not tried again while the scene costs the same. Under auto a scene whose frames take more than twice the interval at the lowest level for 2 seconds stays at poster_time until the choice changes or the scene is loaded again, with the notice of a still scene (quality_auto is still): on the phone the cost model never stops a scene, only the model triangle limit and that measurement do; the desktop also keeps still a scene whose estimate at q0 is over the budget of the tier. The phone settings, the app, the tray and reiwand-ctl quality (from GetState, see dbus.md) show the measured average against the interval and the last decision (model, steady, climbed, dropped, top, held, still, halved or doubled), and --quality auto of reiwand-ctl scene bench and --level auto of the phone bench report the same under auto (frame_ms, period_ms, step, still, pace, pace_fps and every change of level or pace). The pilot decides a pair, the level and a pace divisor, and the interval it judges is the one of the pace in use. The divisor is 1 (the pace of the user's cap or the display) or 2 (half of it: 30 frames a second on a 60 Hz display), and the pace never goes under 30 frames a second, so a cap under 60 stays whole. With playback.auto_pace at detail, the default, a level above that does not fit the whole interval, but whose predicted average fits three quarters of the doubled one, is climbed to at half pace; a level at half pace that averages under half of the whole interval for 6 seconds goes back to the whole pace before any level goes up; and an overload halves the pace before it drops a level while the level is above q0, and drops a level once the pace is already halved. smooth never changes the pace. The same waits, cool-downs and bounce guards apply to the pace (a pace that flips again within two minutes doubles the wait before it returns to whole, up to four times), a level the user fixes never changes its pace, and the user's cap bounds it (half pace halves the cap). On the phone the halved pace presents every second vsync of Choreographer, the skipped ones do no work, and the measured time stays the GPU time of each frame drawn; on the desktop the halved pace halves the rate the daemon schedules the frames of that output at. The frame rate otherwise never depends on the level (60 on battery unless the user sets another cap). It presents through three swapchain images (latency 2), so the GPU draws a frame while the next one is recorded. Two changes are at least 5 seconds apart. The quality only moves on devices that time their frames, which all do, with timestamps or by waiting (see below). GetState().outputs[x].quality and quality_reason tell the level and why it is below the highest; the app, the tray and the phone show both, translated.

The quality the user chooses#

The user can take the level out of these hands with playback.quality (see SetOption in dbus.md): auto, the default, is everything above; q0 to q3 fix the level, and min-q1 or min-q2 keep the automatic choice but never let it go below that level. A level the user sets always holds, also on battery, with power saving, while the device is warm and when the scene costs more than the tier holds: only the frame rate still follows the tier, the power source and the temperature, so a heavy scene shows fewer frames a second instead of fewer details. Above a floor, the automatic choice and the GPU time of the frames move the level as usual. The reason is then user, and when the frames measured at the chosen level take more than 85 % of their interval (before 12 frames are measured: when the level costs more than the budget of the tier) the app (next to the quality choice in the settings and on each screen that shows the scene), the tray, the phone (under its quality setting) and reiwand-ctl quality and reiwand-ctl state warn: the measured time against the interval and the rate it allows (or the cost against the budget), that frames may drop and battery use rise, and whether the automatic choice would lower the level or keep the scene still. Frames measured to fit silence the estimate. Only the automatic choice stops a scene: a scene over budget even at the lowest level (on the desktop), one measured far too slow there, or with more model triangles than the tier draws, stays at poster_time under auto and plays at the chosen level under q0 to q3, and at the floor (or higher, when it fits) under min-q1 and min-q2; clients say so too, and for a scene heavy only in model triangles, whose cost fits, they warn that it draws more model triangles than the device holds and that the automatic choice would keep it still. Only the hard limits that protect memory and the driver stay with every choice: the model data and triangles a scene may load, texture sizes, and the effects left out by the hard budget below or after they stopped the GPU, which the warning counts. The phone keeps its own value, and stopping on battery saver or at a critical temperature still stops it.

The hard budget#

Every view of a scene also holds a hard budget, whatever the quality level: each effect use, built in or packaged, does its units (at the highest level, four times for an effect with a state, which may run four steps a frame) times the share it covers times the megapixels drawn, at least half a unit, and the uses together may do at most 2 000, 4 000, 8 000 or 16 000 of those units a frame on the four tiers; at most 16 uses with a state may run in a scene. The engine adds the uses in the order of the layers, a group before its layers, and leaves out each use that does not fit with the effect <id> on <layer> was left out: the effects of the scene would do more than <limit> units of work a frame on this GPU (or ... the scene has more than 16 effects that keep a state). When the scene loads, before it builds anything, it leaves out only the uses that cannot fit at any size (past the 16 stateful uses, or past the limit at half a unit each), so a package effect whose uses are all left out is never built; it counts the work whenever the size a view draws at changes, at that size and over the part of the canvas the screen shows, so a large canvas shown on a small output keeps the effects that fit there. The state textures of all uses together may hold at most 512 MiB, beside the 512 MiB of textures a frame may borrow; a use whose state would go past it is left out with its state textures would take the scene past 512 MiB. A frame may borrow at most 512 MiB of textures, or four full frames of the output if that is more, and a view holds at most 1 GiB of textures in all, or its states, its cached layers and its target plus the frame's share and two full frames more if that is more (so an 8K output keeps its effects): before it makes another texture it drops the ones it used longest ago, then the cached layers this frame has not drawn, and a use whose texture still does not fit is left out with its textures would take more than the <n> MiB of textures this view may hold (or ... a frame may borrow). Layers draw into textures whose sides grow in steps of an eighth of a power of two (at least 64 pixels), so a zooming layer reuses a few sizes. The first pass that writes a target of a package effect clears it as it draws instead of in a pass of its own, and an adjustment layer reads what is below it directly instead of a copy. reiwand-ctl scene bench prints these notes after its numbers.
