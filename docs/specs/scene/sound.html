{ "blend_space": "canvas", "id": "gusts", "type": "sound", "sound": "wind", "loop": true, "fade_in": 3, "fade_out": 3,
  "play": { "prop": "wind", "map": { "calm": false, "breeze": true } },
  "volume": { "timeline": "storm", "keys": [[0, 0.4], [20, 1, "in_out_sine"], [40, 0.4]] }, "pan": -0.3 }

A sound layer plays a sound asset: .ogg (Vorbis or Opus), .opus, .flac or .wav, mono or stereo, sampled at 8 to 192 kHz, at most 8 MiB and 60 s long, and at most 180 s for all the sounds of a scene together; the package reader checks the headers and the daemon stops decoding a sound that goes on longer than its header says (an MP3 without a length in its header may last up to the 60 s). A scene has at most 8 sound layers, which mix together.

Sound follows the time of the scene. Each layer knows how long ago the scene started it: since the scene started for a play that is always on, since the key that turned it on for play keyframes (on a looping timeline, since the start of the pass), since the trigger for play_on, and since it turned on for the rest. The sound plays at that point of its clip or of its list, so a loop that drifts more than 60 ms from the scene jumps back in step with a 5 ms fade, a scene that resumes plays from where its timelines are, and a sound started again starts over. A layer keyed on a timeline that is paused or has ended keeps playing on, and jumps back to the timeline when it plays again; a timeline started late or sought starts its sounds at its position, not from zero. The engine re-aligns playing sounds with the scene once a second. About 50 ms pass between the scene and the speaker (the soundtrack asks PipeWire for 20 ms periods and keeps about 20 ms queued); positions are counted from the playing position of the audio output, plus one 20 ms PipeWire period when the sound goes to PipeWire, so the queue does not shift sounds.

Field Meaning
loop true (prefilled) repeats the sound without a gap; false plays it once each time play turns on
play starts the sound when it turns on and stops it when it turns off (prefilled true); the sound plays from the point the scene says, see above
volume 0 to 1 (prefilled 1); changes glide over 20 ms, so keyframed curves sound smooth
pan -1 (left) to 1 (right), prefilled 0, gliding like volume
fade_in, fade_out seconds (0 to 60) the sound takes to rise when it starts and to fall when it stops

Newer sound layers may also use (sound2):

Field Meaning
sound as MP3 .mp3 files (MPEG Layer III) whose first frame follows the ID3 tag, checked like the others; only a file named .mp3 is read as MP3
sounds instead of sound, a list of 1 to 16 sound assets
mode with sounds: sequence (prefilled, in order) or random (picked with the scene seed)
pause [min, max] seconds (0 to 600) of silence between clips, picked with the scene seed
stream true plays the files as they are read instead of from memory, for long sounds: at most 192 MiB and 3 hours each and 384 MiB for all the streamed sounds of a scene, outside the 180 s of the others
play_on a trigger (start, beat, prop:<id>, event:<name>, click:<layer>, enter:<layer>, leave:<layer> or media:<what>, as for timelines) that starts the sound, always under the user's volume and mute

A layer has either sound or sounds. With loop: false, sequence plays the list once and random one clip each time it starts. random never plays the same clip twice in a row when the list has more than one, and the clips and pauses it picks follow the scene seed, so the same scene with a fixed clock.seed plays the same order. A streamed sound is decoded a little ahead of where it plays, and the next streamed clip of a list starts decoding 1.5 s before its turn, so lists and loops of long sounds play without gaps; nothing of a stream is kept once it has played. play_on starts the sound again each time its trigger fires; play still mutes it while it is off. The desktop and the phone play these sounds (the phone decodes them with the codecs of the system when they first sound, reads a stream sound as it plays and keeps about 0.2 s between the scene and the speaker).

play, volume and pan may be literals or follow a user property or keyframes on the scene clock, which a timeline triggered by a property or a beat can drive; they never follow the audio analysis, the pointer, a clock text or a script, so a scene cannot hear its own sound. Values that change over time are read at the frame rate of the output without rendering a frame. A sound layer draws nothing, takes none of the visual fields (transform, opacity, effects and so on) and cannot be a mask.

Every scene with sound has a volume user property, a slider from 0 to 100 % (prefilled 100) added automatically; a manifest may declare it to change its label, prefilled or order, as long as it stays a slider within 0 to 100. Scenes start muted like videos and follow playback.muted and playback.volume; only one output plays the sound of a scene shown on several outputs, and nothing plays on the lock screen (see "Scene sound" in dbus.md). On the phone the sound plays only while the wallpaper is shown on the home screen (not on the lock screen or in a preview), pauses with the scene when battery saver, a low battery or heat stop it, follows playback.duck and releases its audio output 5 s after every layer has stopped; a scene that has sound layers gets its volume setting there too. The settings of the app set playback.volume with a slider in steps of 5 % under the sound switch; the output asks for the power-saving mode of AAudio and the timing of the sound subtracts the frames it still holds.
