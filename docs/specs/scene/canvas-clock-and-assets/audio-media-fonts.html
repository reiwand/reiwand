Scene audio and newer asset fields#

The scene may also set audio: { "attack" (0.001 to 2 s), "release" (0.01 to 10 s), "gain_release" (0.05 to 10 s) }, a block that may be absent and that carries all three fields when present, how fast audio values rise and fall and how the relative reference recovers (audio_response). With the relative kind of audio binding every band is measured against the loudest band of the last moments; gain_release is the time in which that reference falls toward the current level after a loud sound (a scene without an audio block follows the 5 s reference of the sound system, and the editor prefills 5 when it adds the block): a short one makes bands dip right after each hit and recover before the next one. The reference is kept by the scene runtime from the unnormalised band powers of each audio frame (AudioFrame.power), so scripts' and shaders' band() and every binding see the same values; the audio frames also carry the reference of the sound system for scenes without an audio block. The editor shows it as a slider next to attack and release, and the band meter of the sound panel draws the bands against that reference live.

Sheets, pictures and videos#

An image layer that shows a sheet plays its frames on the scene clock, so that every layer showing the same sheet at the same rate shows the same frame; its playback sets the rate, the range, whether it loops and the frame (layer_playback). A sheet without fps or durations shows its first frame. The scene asks for a frame only when the next frame of a shown sheet is due, never while the layer is hidden, and the lowest quality level shows the first frame of the range without moving.

Each picture is decoded once and uploaded once per GPU, and every screen that shows the scene draws from that one texture. Once a picture is on the GPU its decoded copy leaves main memory; the engine reads it again from the package only when no screen holds the texture any more. Every shader and pipeline a scene needs is built when the scene loads, never during a frame, and a frame hands the textures of a finished layer to the next layer that needs one of the same size, so a steady frame creates nothing and makes no allocations of its own. Evaluating a frame allocates nothing either: Runtime::frame_in takes the lists of the previous frame back (Frame::spare). On the phone a scene script that changes layers each frame still makes a few allocations per changed layer on the drawing thread (the copy of its answer and the layer names it reads back: about 15 a frame for the four planets of the orbits example); the phone test bounds them at 16 a frame.

Each video asset has one decoder, shared by every layer, mask and effect that shows it on every screen. The first layer of the scene that shows the asset, as a video layer or as the picture of an image layer, steers it with its playback: rate (a video plays from 0.1 to 4 times, and its time, its end and what scripts read follow the rate it plays at; 0 holds the video), playing, loop (prefilled on), start and end in seconds. The time of a video runs on the scene clock while it plays and something shows it: a video that no visible layer, mask or effect shows waits where it is and its decoder pauses, as for a paused or finished video, and none of them asks for frames. Without loop the video stops at end, or at its own end (read from the header of its WebM, MKV or MP4 file), and tells scripts. On battery its decoder runs no faster than the frame rate of the scene, which clock.max_fps caps. On the phone the decoder is the platform's MediaCodec, through the system player, which plays only the part between start and end.

On the desktop at most 16 video decoders run at once, for all screens together (the video of a wallpaper, the videos of scenes, own clocks and videos that scripts show). Clocks that play the same file in step (the same part, speed, play state and sound level, no seek of a script, and less than 0.1 s apart) share one decoder. A video that would need a decoder past the limit shows a still frame of its video, the one at its start, scaled to at most 1920 pixels a side, and the daemon writes a warning that says how many videos show a still; a decoder that runs keeps running, and a video showing a still plays again as soon as a decoder is free.

Fonts#

Fonts may also be TrueType or OpenType collections (.ttc, .otc) and compressed .woff2 files (fonts2). Videos may be used as masks, effect textures and the picture of a spot light (video_textures), and wherever a picture is; sounds may also be MP3 (see "Sound"). Pictures may also be KTX2 textures (ktx2, see "KTX2 textures"). Meshes and models are binary glTF 2.0 files (.glb, at most 64 MiB each; FBX or OBJ convert to it with common 3D tools): readers read only the header and the JSON part of each file once (at most 4 MiB, and 16 MiB for all the models of a scene), refuse files that load anything from outside themselves or require an extension other than KHR_materials_emissive_strength, KHR_materials_unlit, KHR_mesh_quantization and KHR_texture_transform (model layers draw the offset, rotation, scale and texCoord of each map, read the first two sets of texture coordinates, TEXCOORD_0 and TEXCOORD_1, and sample each texture with the wrap (repeat, clamp or mirror) and the filters (nearest or linear, with or without mipmaps) of its sampler; the editor warns about a map that reads a third set, drawn with the first), skins of more than 256 bones, more than 64 blend shapes per primitive, more than 65 536 nodes or 4096 meshes, materials, animations or skins, more than 262 144 list entries for all the models of a scene (buffer views have a cap of their own, 262 144 per file, and do not count there), pictures shown by textures whose distinct byte ranges together pass the size of the file (pictures no texture shows are never read, and a model keeps its pictures as ranges of the file it read instead of copies) and more than 2 000 000 triangles, and read the names of its nodes (attachment points), skin joints (bones), meshes (parts), materials, blend shapes (extras.targetNames, or shape_<n>) and animations with their length, against which every name the scene gives is checked. Unnamed elements are called node_<n>, mesh_<n>, material_<n> and animation_<n>.
