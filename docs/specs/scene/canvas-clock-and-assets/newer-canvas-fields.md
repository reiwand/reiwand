# Newer canvas fields

The fields below came after the first version of the scene format. Each one names a feature of
`requires.features` (the table in `package.md`), which `reiwand-ctl pack build` fills in; an engine
that cannot draw a feature yet refuses the whole package with `this package needs a newer version
of Reiwand: it uses the feature …` instead of drawing it wrongly or leaving it out, and keeps
its thumbnail and details in the library. Scenes without them read and draw as before.

| `canvas` field | Meaning | Feature |
|----------------|---------|---------|
| `parallax` | `{ "amount" (0 to 4, prefilled 1), "delay" (0 to 2 s, prefilled 0.15), "pointer" (0 to 1, prefilled 1), "offset" ([x, y], −1 to 1 each, prefilled [0, 0]), "gyro" }`; `amount`, `pointer` and `offset` may be bound like any value, so a property, a timeline or the sound may move the parallax while the pointer still moves it too (`parallax_values`): how far and how softly layers with a `parallax` follow the pointer, how much the pointer counts and whether the tilt of the phone moves them too (on Android, through the same motion sensors as picture parallax, only while the scene is shown; the phone has no pointer, so there every scene with parallax layers follows the tilt as if `gyro` were on, or the launcher pages when the phone has no motion sensor, and neither moves them with the battery saver on); `delay` is how softly the eased pointer settles: each axis follows the pointer (in fractions of the canvas, 0.5 the middle, the middle when there is no pointer) as a critically damped spring with ω = 4 / `delay` per second (`delay` 0 counts as 0.001 s, so it follows at once); each frame steps by dt, kept within 0 to 0.1 s, with x = ω·dt and the decay d = 1 / (1 + x + 0.48·x² + 0.235·x³) (close to e^(−x)): with the gap c = eased − pointer and the speed v, p = (v + ω·c)·dt, then v becomes (v − ω·p)·d and the eased value pointer + (c + p)·d; once the gap is under 0.0001 and the speed under 0.001 per second it snaps onto the pointer with no speed. From rest a jump of the pointer is followed as 1 − (1 + ω·t)·e^(−ω·t), so after `delay` seconds about 9 % of it is left (5·e^(−4) ≈ 0.092); the prefilled 0.15 s is the easing the pointer already has, so it adds no second spring; `pointer: 0` needs no pointer; a layer moves by its `parallax` times the canvas size times `amount` times the offset of the pointer from the middle (−0.5 to 0.5) times `pointer`, plus half the tilt and half the `offset`, so `offset` `[1, 0]` moves layers as the pointer at the right edge would; scripts may set `amount`, `delay`, `pointer` and `offset` of `scene.parallax`; where the system asks for reduced motion, layers stay in place and neither the pointer nor the tilt moves them | `parallax2` |
| `camera` | `{ "center", "zoom" (0.1 to 10), "rotation", "shake" }` moves, zooms and turns the whole scene as one transform without extra passes: the point `center` of the canvas (prefilled its middle) shows at the middle, `zoom` times larger and turned clockwise by `rotation` degrees; `shake` is `{ "enabled", "amplitude" (0 to 1000 px), "roughness" (0 to 1), "speed" (0 to 60 Hz) }`, a smooth noise of the scene time (four octaves, each weighed by `roughness` against the one before) that moves the scene by at most `amplitude` pixels and asks for a frame every frame while it runs; it is turned off where motion is reduced and at the lowest quality level, and the scene then stands still; all of them may be bound | `camera` |
| `bloom` | `{ "threshold" (0 to 4), "strength" (0 to 8), "radius" (0 to 128 px), "radius_unit" (`canvas` or `screen`), "tint", "knee" (0 to 1), "saturation" (0 to 4), "limit" (0 to 16, or `null` for no cap), "hdr": { "threshold" (0 to 16), "strength" (0 to 8), "knee" (0 to 1), "levels" (1 to 8), "scatter" (0 to 2) } }`, every field written, a glow of the bright parts drawn after everything else, with the `bloom` effect and its `tint` over the whole canvas, as an adjustment layer at the top would; the `hdr` threshold and strength replace the plain ones at level `q3`, and at `q2` on mains power; `knee` (the editor prefills 0.5) is the share of the threshold over which the cut softens, 0 being a hard cut that leaves what lies under the threshold exactly untouched; `saturation` (1) mixes the bright parts with their grey (0.2989 r + 0.587 g + 0.114 b) before the blur, 0 giving a grey glow and 4 a vivid one; `limit` caps each channel of the bright parts times the strength before the blur, so that a very bright pixel cannot outshine a weaker one beyond that value, and `null` leaves it uncapped; `radius_unit` `screen` reads `radius` in pixels of the screen instead of the canvas, using the scale of the canvas to the screen, so a canvas twice the size of the screen keeps the same halo on it; `hdr.levels` adds wider blurs, each twice the radius of the one before, summed with the weight `scatter` raised to its level (`scatter` 0 or `levels` 1 give exactly the plain glow); together they make the wide halo of a light brighter than white on an `hdr` canvas: with 8 levels the glow falls with the distance d faster than 1/d at `scatter` 1, about as 1/d at 1.5 and slower at 2, reaches about `radius` times 2 to the power of `levels` (at a radius of 8 it still lights the canvas 320 pixels away) and, being made of what passes the threshold, a brightness of 2 over a threshold of 1 spills about ten times the light of 1.1), and the quality caps them: 1 level at Q0, 2 at Q1, 4 at Q2 and up to 8 at Q3, each extra level costing about a third of the one before at its size (at most 7 more passes of two blurs, then the sum); it costs like that effect (the bright parts at half size, the blur at a quarter, and the glow added onto the canvas by blending, without a copy of the canvas), is dropped where the effect is, is left out while its strength is zero and is drawn only when something below it changed | `bloom` |
| `hdr`, `tonemap` | `hdr: true` keeps colors brighter than white in 16-bit intermediate images (in either blend space, on every tier and on phones, wherever the graphics card can draw and blend them; cards that cannot keep 8 bits and still apply the tone map) and draws models in 16 bits, and `tonemap` (`none`, `aces`, `agx` or `srgb_to_linear`, only with `hdr`) maps them to the screen in one last pass at every quality level, after the `bloom` glow is added; `none` keeps the picture of a scene without `hdr`. `srgb_to_linear` (`tonemap_srgb_to_linear`) reads the color the screen would show, glow included, once more through the sRGB curve: with `c` that color per channel (encoded, 0 to 1), the screen shows `clamp(lin(c), 0, 1)`, `lin(v) = v / 12.92` up to `v = 0.04045` and `((v + 0.055) / 1.055)^2.4` above, so a scene of 0.5 under a glow of 0.5 shows `lin(1.0) = 1.0`. On a screen with HDR output (`playback.hdr` in `dbus.md`, `COLOR_MODE_HDR` and an extended-range surface on phones with Android 14 or later) the last pass keeps 16 bits and, instead of `aces` or `agx` (after the curve of `srgb_to_linear`, without its clamp), bends what is brighter than 0.8 of white softly toward the peak of that screen (its brightest white over its reference white), keeping hue and midtones; `none` passes the colors on | `hdr` |
| `clear` | `false` draws every frame over the previous one, which leaves trails: the canvas is filled with `background` only when the view starts or changes size, and no layer is kept as a still picture; each frame that changes is drawn whole instead of only the part that changed, so it costs more; the `bloom` glow and the darkening of the screen layout are drawn over a copy of what is kept, each frame anew, so they never pile up into it | `keep_frame` |
| `perspective` | distance of the viewer in pixels (100 to 100000) for layers tilted with `transform.tilt` and moved with `transform.z`; `null` leaves the distance at which the canvas height fills the `fov` of `camera3d`, or 45° | `tilt` |
| `ambient`, `sky` | colors of the light around the scene and from above, for lit layers | `ambient_light` |
| `wind` | `[x, y]` pixels per second, may be bound: the wind that moves bones with `physics` and models with the `foliage` material | `bone_physics` |
| `gusts` | 0 to 1 (prefilled 0.35): how much of `wind` comes and goes in gusts, from a steady wind at 0 to one that drops to nothing between gusts at 1 | `bone_physics` |
