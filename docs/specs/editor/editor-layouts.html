The editor comes with six layouts, in this order and under Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+6 (benchfactory.rs; the shares are those of a 1920 × 1008 workbench). Each holds only the panels its kind of work uses; every other panel starts closed (in its closed list) and Window, the canvas bar's "Open … again" or anything that brings a panel into view (a click on the problems of the status bar, the curve button…) opens it where Window would:

Layout Tree
Scene Assets and Scripts (300) · the canvas over Timeline and Problems (168) · Inspector and Effects (500) over Layers (380)
Particles Layers (360) over Particles (320) · the canvas over Curves and Console (270) · Inspector (600) over Cost and See as (390)
Scripts Scripts (280) · Code and Reference over Console and Problems (250) · the canvas (392) over Inputs and Try (580)
Simulation Try (560) over Screens and Problems (300) · the canvas · See as (420) over Inputs (400)
Animation a row of Layers and Assets (300), the canvas and Inspector and Effects (380), 540 high, over a row of Timeline and Curves (560)
Wallpaper Options and Texts and languages (300) · Wallpaper sheet · How it will look (260) over the canvas (400) over Review and Problems (420)

A panel a layout keeps closed comes back next to the panels it is with in the layout in use or, when that layout does not hold it, where the full tree of Scene (benchfull.rs, which places every panel once) puts it: with its companions there, or on its side.

The top bar shows the six, each a word with its blueprint (18 × 12 px) and its name in Red Hat Display, the one in use in Frost and bold with the accent mark under it; when one of the author's layouts is in use it follows them. When the bar is short of room only the layout in use keeps its name. A click on a layout, or its key, changes the workbench to it; Ctrl+7, Ctrl+8 and Ctrl+9 choose the author's first three layouts. Each layout keeps the changes the author makes to it (moved, stacked, resized or closed panels), apart from the others, and "Reset this layout" (Window and the canvas bar's "More") gives back the layout in use as it came, or, for one of the author's, as it was saved. "Save as new layout…" (SceneLayoutName) asks for a name and keeps the workbench as it is under it, as a layout of the author's that is then in use; a name is refused, with its reason in the dialog, when it is empty, starts or ends with a space or is the name of another of the author's layouts. "Rename or remove your layouts…" (SceneLayoutOwn) lists them with their blueprints, "Rename…" and "Remove"; "Remove" asks again in its row ("Remove it", coral, or "Cancel"), since it cannot be undone, and removing the layout in use goes back to Scene. A scene just made opens in Scene; a scene opened again opens in the layout chosen last (the choice belongs to the window, not to the scene). A web, image, video or preset package opens in Wallpaper (benchkits::opening), in the same workbench (see "Packages that are not scenes").

The rest is the workbench built by SceneDocks: a tree whose inner nodes are splits (a row or a column, with a share of its length for each child) and whose leaves are tab groups (SceneGroup), each with its panels and the one it shows. There are twenty-three panels: the canvas (SceneStage, with the canvas bar at the top and, if the scene has no layers, the card "Add a layer" with image, text, time or date and particles, below the notices and the canvas bar, scrolled when the group is too short for it), Layers, Assets, Options, Try, the Inspector (SceneFields), Effects (SceneEffectsPanel), Timeline (SceneFoot), Curves (SceneCurves), Problems, See as (SceneLookPanel), Inputs (SceneInputs), Screens (SceneScreensPanel), the Wallpaper sheet (SceneSheet), Texts and languages (SceneTextsPanel), How it will look (SceneCoverPanel), Review (SceneReviewPanel), Particles, Cost, Scripts, Code, Reference and Console (see their sections); the tree is that of the layout in use (see "Layouts": the panels it holds; the rest are closed), and the usual layout of a split is that of the layout in use as it came. Each panel is a unit (SceneUnit) created once and reassigned to the body of the group that holds it, so it keeps its state when it moves. The canvas is alone in its group and is never stacked with other panels; its strip shows the tab "Canvas", which cannot be dragged but has the tab menu (menu key, Shift+F10 or right click): "Move left", "Move right", "Move up" and "Move down" trade its place with what is next to it that way (benchcanvas.rs: at the nearest split of that direction, with its column and share), "Detach into a window" takes it to a window of its own, and "Stack with…", "Detach the whole group" and "Close the panel" are off for it. A tab strip is 32 px high, darker than the group, with each tab an icon and its name (13 px) and a line after it; the current tab takes the colour of the group and the mark of place, in the accent only in the group that has the keyboard focus. The tabs stay on one line: those that do not fit go, in their order, into the "⋯" at the end of the strip (the tab shown always keeps its place), which then lists them with "Move or close …" for the tab shown; otherwise "⋯" opens the menu of the tab the group shows. Each group clips what it holds below its strip, with the room of a focus ring around the body, so nothing a panel lays out draws over the strip or another group.

Three things change what the workbench shows without changing the layout or saving anything. F11 (and "Maximize (F11)" in the tab menu) enlarges the panel that holds the keyboard to the whole workbench, without edges, and F11 again gives the workbench back. "Detach into a window" in the tab menu takes the panel (never the canvas) out of the workbench into a window of its own, with "Return to the workbench"; closing the window returns it too, to its place in the layout, and the window keeps its size for the next time the same panels are detached, also after the app is closed (editor.conf, [windows] sizes: JSON, the panels joined with "+" to [width, height], written half a second after the size settles; not read or written during the checks; on Wayland the desktop chooses where it appears). Below 1500 px of window width, when the first child of the layout is a side without the canvas, that side folds into a rail along the left edge, as wide as a tab strip, with one icon per panel; a click (or showing the panel from a menu or the status bar) unfolds it over the workbench in a group of its own, beside the rail, and a click again folds it; the rail's edge does not move. The status bar lists F6 and F11 among the keys of every panel. F6 and Shift+F6 take the keyboard to the tab shown in the next or previous group, in the order of the tree (left to right, top to bottom; the rail is skipped); Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab show the next or previous tab of the group that has the keyboard; Esc folds a panel unfolded from the rail. Window has "Detach into a window" and "Maximize" (or "Back to the workbench"), with F11 beside it, for the panel that has the keyboard.

The tree, its operations and its sizes are in Rust (apps/reiwand-app/src/bench*.rs, the QML type Workbench); SceneLayout holds the tree in use. Each group has a floor (the widest floor of its panels: the canvas sideRight by half of it, the Inspector sideRight, the others sideLeft, and two canvas bars of height, or, for the timeline while it is unfolded, its tab strip and the whole timeline); a split gives each child its share and takes what a child needs to reach its floor from the others in proportion to what they have above theirs; when the floors do not fit, the child that holds the canvas keeps its floor (or half of the split, whichever is smaller) and the others shrink below theirs in proportion to their floors. A group that shows the folded timeline takes exactly its tab strip and the timeline bar, and its edge does not move. The edges between the children of a split (SceneGrip) are dragged, moved with the arrows (16 px) and returned with a double click or Enter to the place they have in the usual layout (to the middle of the two when the usual layout has no such split); a drag never takes a child below its floor. In a group too narrow for the timeline bar on one row (SceneTimeBar), its easing and keyframe buttons go to a second row under play, the time and the playhead slider.

A tab is dragged to a new place (SceneDrop). Over the group under the pointer it draws a cross of five targets (SceneDropTarget, each a small plan of the part it takes): the middle one stacks the panel in that group as its last tab, and the four around it split the group in two, halves of equal share, with the panel on that side; letting go anywhere else on a group stacks it too. At the middle of each edge of the workbench a target opens the panel as a new group along that whole edge (a quarter of it). Only targets that change the layout are drawn: the canvas group has no middle target, the panel's own group has none either and offers its sides only when it holds other panels. Before the drop, the zone that the panel will take is painted with the place it will have after the drop (worked out from the tree it would make), with "With …", "Left of …", "Right of …", "Above …", "Below …" or "Along this edge of the window". The tab menu (menu key, Shift+F10 or right click, SceneTabMenu) offers "Move left", "Move right", "Move up" and "Move down": the panel joins the nearest group on that side in the tree, passing over the canvas; if it passed over the canvas and nothing lies beyond, it opens as a new group along that edge of the whole workbench (a quarter of it); if nothing lies on that side, it splits its group in two and opens in the half of that side; when none of this applies (alone in a group at the edge) the choice is disabled. "Stack with…" lists the other groups but the canvas, named by their panels, and the panel joins the one chosen as its last tab. "Close the panel" takes it out of the workbench (the canvas is never closed); for each closed panel the canvas bar's "More" shows "Open … again", which brings it back as the last tab of the first panel of its usual group that is open, or, when none is, alone on its usual side: along the left or right edge of the workbench (a quarter of it) or below the canvas (a quarter of the canvas). A group left without panels closes and a split left with one child gives its place to it; "Window › Reset this layout" (also in "More") goes back to the layout as it came, with its panels open.

The layouts are a view preference, with no editor action or reiwand-ctl command. They are saved, 400 ms after the last change, in ~/.config/reiwand/workbench.json as {"format": 8, "content": {"chosen", "factory", "own"}} (benchdesk.rs, benchstore.rs): chosen is the layout in use, {"factory": "scene"} (or particles, scripts, simulation, animation, wallpaper) or {"own": "<name>"}; factory holds the six layouts by those names, each a workbench {"root", "closed"} with the author's changes; own lists the author's layouts in their order, each {"name", "saved", "view"}, where saved is the workbench as it was saved (what "Reset this layout" gives back) and view the one in use. In a workbench closed lists the closed panels and each node is {"split": {"dir", "shares", "nodes"}} or {"group": {"panels", "shown"}}. Every field is written, like every format (explicit-values.md). Reading refuses a file of another format, a missing field (named by its path, content.factory.scene.root.split.nodes[0].group.shown), a tree that breaks the rules (a split of fewer than two nodes, shares that are not one per node, above 0 and adding up to 1, a split inside another of the same direction, an empty group, a panel twice or neither in a group nor closed, the canvas with other panels or closed, a shown panel that is not in its group), a name of the author's that is empty, starts or ends with a space or is taken twice, and a chosen layout of the author's that is not in own, naming the place. An older file is brought up a format at a time (benchmend.rs, benchstore.rs): format 1 (one tree) gets an empty closed; format 2 (one workbench, {"root", "closed"}) becomes the Scene layout, the five others as they come; formats 3, 4 and 5 get Effects and Curves, then See as, Inputs and Screens, then the four panels of the wallpaper closed in every workbench whose tree lacks them, and formats 6 and 7 as described below. A file of this format that does not read whole is read layout by layout: a broken layout of the six goes back to how it comes, a broken one of the author's is left out, a chosen layout that is gone becomes Scene, the others are kept, and the notice names what was lost; the file is written again with the next change. Only a file that is not a set of layouts at all, or of a newer format, is refused: the editor opens with the six layouts as they come, in Scene, shows the reason in its notice and does not write over the file until "Reset this layout", which writes them. A missing file is the six layouts as they come, but for the Scene layout while the arrangement key that earlier versions kept in window.conf (group editor: {"places": {"left", "right", "bottom"}, "sizes"}) describes one (benchold.rs): its left, right and bottom panels in groups on those sides of the canvas, at their sizes (within a third of the workbench), with the panels it did not know closed; panels it names twice or does not know are left out, and one that names none gives the Scene layout as it comes. Those layouts are written to the file at once, and only then is the key removed from window.conf (its other keys and lines stay, written beside it first); a key that cannot be removed is converted again next time, to the same layout, and a file that cannot be written leaves the key where it is.

During a drag of a layer or a handle over the canvas (action layer with grip or transform, as a gesture), each step applies the action, sends the scene to the preview and publishes only the handles (gizmos, from outline::moving); the scene outline (view) and the property rows are not rebuilt. They are rebuilt once when the pointer is released (hold) or when the drag stays still for 120 ms, and the drag remains a single undo step. What the saved scene needs is saved first and says so ("Saved before setting it as the wallpaper.", "Saved before publishing.").

"View as wallpaper" asks on which screen when there is more than one, minimizes the window and leaves a fixed system notification (org.freedesktop.Notifications, without a timeout) with "Back to the editor", "Keep it" (sets it as the wallpaper for real) and "Stop the preview". If the view ends by itself (the daemon cuts it or crashes), the notification changes to "The wallpaper view ended" with "View it again". Without a notification service the window returns and the notice stays inside it. "Set as wallpaper" asks for the screen the same way and notifies ""Title" is your wallpaper." with "Undo", which sets back what each screen had. The Assets sections (scripts, templates, effects and shape groups) fold and remember their state between sessions.
