Layer outlines on the canvas

The canvas draws the outline of each layer where the scene draws it, with its parents, groups, anchor, origin, skew, parallax and 2D camera, from the editor query seats::seats, which places every layer with the same code as the renderer (Runtime::seats of reiwand-scene: the frame its position lives in and its whole transform); groups get the box around their layers. The selected layer gets handles: the corners scale it from its pivot (Shift keeps the proportions), the middles of the edges stretch one side or, with Ctrl, skew it along that edge, a round handle above turns it, the dot of the pivot moves its origin without moving the layer, and a depth gizmo drawn in the perspective of the scene tilts and lifts it: a dashed ring around the pivot in the plane of the layer, the upright axis whose end tilts it around the width (transform.tilt[0]), the level axis whose end tilts it around the height (tilt[1]) and a line out of the layer from the middle of its bottom edge, along the direction z moves it, whose diamond lifts it (transform.z). The gizmo comes from seats::depth (the outline field Gizmo.depth: pivot, ring, height, width, foot, lift and reach, its length in canvas pixels, 0.35 of the shorter side of the layer, from 40 to 400), which projects every point with the plane the renderer leans the layer by (Seated.plane of Runtime::seats, Plane::lean, the same code as the scene draws with). The tilt grip ({ "tilt": { "axis": 0 } } or 1) picks the angle, −89 to 89 degrees, that puts the end of that axis nearest to the pointer, the one closest to the current angle when two are as near, and lift the depth that puts the end of the line nearest to it. Each handle sends the grip of the layer action with the point it is dragged to, so the corner or edge it holds stays under the pointer whatever the parents do. With Magnets (on by default) a dragged layer snaps to the edges and the middle of the canvas and of the other layers, showing a guide where it snaps, and Alt places it freely; the arrow keys nudge the selected layer by a pixel, by ten with Shift, as one step per press. The canvas takes the keyboard focus (the SceneFocus signal) when a layer is picked in the layer list, when a layer is tapped or dragged and when the empty canvas is tapped, so the arrows work at once; a change of the selection that is not one of those gestures leaves the focus where it is. Fit to the canvas covers the canvas with it, fits it inside or stretches it (fit). Follow a layer picks its parent with a tap on another layer of the canvas, keeping it where it is (parent with keep), and Stop following removes it the same way; while a layer follows another a dashed line joins their pivots, and the list of the parent field of the inspector only offers the layers it may follow, without the ones that would close a loop or pass 8 layers (seats::parents). Other tools of the canvas build on the same place: Seat::at takes a point of the box of a layer, in fractions, to the canvas, and Seat::fraction brings it back; the overlay SceneSeat.qml does the same for a layer named by id and sends the grips. A tilted or lifted layer, or one under a tilted parent, also gets a dashed outline where the scene projects it (Gizmo.corners, through the same perspective as the scene), and its handles sit where the scene shows them (Gizmo.handles: the projected corners, middles of the edges and pivot). The grips bring the pointer back onto the layer: turn, scale, skew and size onto the plane of the layer (Seat::unlean), and move and origin onto the plane the pivot moves in, the layer at its depth without its own tilt (Seat::unlift), so whatever they hold lands exactly under the pointer. Taps, drags and the hover of a layer take only the points inside its outline (the same polygon the canvas draws, so a skewed layer is hit exactly as drawn), not the rectangle around it. The 2D camera button of the toolbar adds the camera of the scene (canvas.camera) or removes it, and switches between View through the camera, where the canvas and every handle show the scene as the screen does (seats::seats places the layers through the camera), and View the whole canvas with the camera frame, where the preview and the handles leave the camera out and the frame of what the camera shows is drawn over the canvas, from the outline field camera2d (seats::lens::frame: center, zoom, rotation, corners, the point top of the knob that turns it, and whole; null without a camera), computed from the camera the renderer itself uses (Runtime::lens of reiwand-scene) without its shake. The frame has a knob in the middle that moves the camera, one per corner that zooms it and one above that turns it, each through the camera_grip of the canvas action, so a keyed camera gets a key at the playhead or, with Overwrite, its plain value. A script that moves the camera through scene.camera still moves the preview in either view. The inspector of a layer shows its parallax depth (parallax) with a slider per side, across and up and down, or one for both while Same depth across and up and down is on, and a Parallax range of ±1, ±3 or ±10 that widens by itself to hold the stored value; each drag is one step of the depth of the layer action. While the canvas has parallax, Try the parallax in the toolbar puts a simulated pointer on the canvas (the outline field probe), and, with Follow the tilt of a phone, a square whose place from the middle of the canvas is the tilt; dragging them sends probe and the outlines and handles of every layer shift by its depth, and the grips keep working on the shifted layers. While it is on, the chosen layer gets a handle at its middle displaced by its depth (a quarter of the canvas width is 1, from -10 to 10; the same depth across and up and down moves along the diagonal and Shift sets them apart, a depth that follows a binding or keys has none), and choosing the canvas gives its parallax a handle for offset (from -1 to 1 of half the canvas on each side); both send depth of layer and parallax of canvas as drags. The preview stream itself still follows the real pointer. The toolbar also offers guides for 16:9, 21:9, 32:9, 4:3, 16:10 and 9:16 screens. Screens previews the scene spread over several screens: two or three side by side, two stacked, or the user's own layout as the daemon reports it (GetState().outputs[x].mm). It frames the part of the canvas that each screen shows and darkens what the frames between them (layout.bezel_mm) and the edges of the layout hide, and its Spread over every screen box sets or removes display.span: "all-outputs" in the manifest, as an undoable step. The parts come from the editor query {"spread": {"layout", "bezel", "monitors"}} (rigs::query), which answers {spread, canvas, screens, bounds, parts: [{id, rect}]} with each rect in canvas pixels, or error for an unknown layout or screens without a name or a size (at most 64). reiwand-ctl scene preview DIR --layout dual|triple|vertical|mine --out FILE [--time S] writes the same view as a picture, without a desktop: the scene at its poster time, or at second S of the scene act DIR canvas--time is 0 or more (as the editor shows the moment its preview is at), each screen's part at its physical place and the frames between them. Dragging a layer that is not locked selects it and moves it, as one gesture. The handles write with the layer action, as reiwand-ctl scene act DIR layer does: a value with keyframes takes a keyframe at the playhead instead, and so does each part of a position or scale keyed on its own (tracks), while its plain parts take the new value; a part that follows a link refuses the drag and says so. Write over keyframes in the canvas toolbar makes them write a fixed value over keyframes and links (replace). A drag that starts outside the layers, or any drag with Shift, draws a rectangle that selects the layers it touches and picks them as Ctrl does in the layer list, ready for Group. Ctrl and the wheel zoom, the wheel or a drag with the middle or right button pans.
