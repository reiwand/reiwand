# Scripts, templates and assets

| Action | Arguments | Command |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| `import_script` | `file`, a `.js` module (optional with a `template`, which names the new module); copies it to `scripts/<name>` and declares or replaces its entry in `scripts` with the `events` it exports or handles with `on` and the `uses` it imports or calls (`geometry` for `scene.mesh`, `hierarchy` for `setParent`, `children`, `world` and `parent`, `shortcuts` for `openShortcut`, `spawn` and `order`, `effect`, `console`, `camera` for `scene.camera`, `camera3d`, `parallax`, `bloom`, `background`, `ambient` and `sky`, read as whole names or taken apart with `const { parallax } = scene`, `animations` for the `.video` of a layer and for `on("ended")`, `shared` also for imports of other modules), never its `params`, which are written by hand, refusing scripts that would take the scene past 1 MiB or 64 modules; the author checks what was proposed. With `template` (`empty`; `media`, a module that handles `mediaStatus`, `mediaPlayback`, `mediaProperties`, `mediaThumbnail` and `mediaTimeline` and exports `nowPlaying`, `accentColor`, `shown` and `progress` for values to follow with `fn`; `touch`, whose `update` returns 1 while the pointer is over the output and exports `position` and `pressed`; or `timer`, which counts seconds with `setInterval` and exports `seconds`) it writes that module instead, named `file` or, when `file` is omitted, `<template>.js` (`<template>-2.js` when taken), refused when the file exists; the assets panel offers it as New script…, and lists the scripts of the scene with their size, a view of their source, Open to edit them in another app and Remove | `import-script DIR [FILE] [--template empty\|media\|touch\|timer]` |
| `script` | one of `add` (`path`: a `.js` file of the package that the scene does not list yet; declares it like an import), `remove` (`path`; refused, naming each place, while a value follows the module with `script`), `move` (`path`, `to`: the place in the list, 0 first, which is the order the modules run in), `declare` (`path`, optional `params`: sets `events` and `uses` to what the code uses, keeping the `uses` that code cannot show (`clicks`, `geometry`, `animations`, `shortcuts`, `hierarchy`) and the `params`; with `params` the settings the code reads as `params.name` and the module does not declare are added as numbers), `write` (`path` of a listed module, `text`; saves the code and leaves its declaration alone, so the preview reloads and the check shows what changed; it is a step of the history: the editor keeps each version in its state folder, records the one it wrote under `scene.ext.editor.code`, and undo and redo write the recorded version back, only for the modules that step touched, so a file changed outside the editor is never overwritten by an unrelated undo) or `param` (`path`, `id`, `type` (`number`, `bool`, `text`, `color` or `vec2`), `default`, `min` and `max` for numbers, `to` to rename it, taking the `params` of the values that follow the module along, or `remove`; a new setting starts from the default of its type, a change of type drops `min` and `max`, and the default must fit the range; at most 32 settings) | Not a command of its own: `scene act DIR script JSON`, the generic entry for every action |
| `template` | one of `make` (`layers`, one or more layers of the scene, optional `name`: copies a picture, text, shape, solid, spectrum, mesh or group as it is into `templates` under the name, or `<id>_template`, and several layers as one group of copies; the layers stay), `rename` (`name`, `to`, optional `rewrite`: with it the exact string literals `"name"`, `'name'` and `` `name` `` of the scene's scripts become the new name, as one undoable step; the other lines that name it are never rewritten, and `templates::mentions` lists them with file and line) or `remove` (`name`) | Not a command of its own: `scene act DIR template JSON` |
| `import_assets` | `package`, a `.wpak` of kind `assets`, and `parts`, each `layer:ID`, `particles:ID`, `script:PATH` or `effect:ID` (every part when empty); copies the parts with what they name and their files under `assets/<id>-<version>/`, renames ids in use, credits the files with their license, attribution and `source`, and adds the package to `origin.derived_from` (see "Shared parts"); a scene of the first version is refused | `import-assets DIR PACKAGE [--part KIND:ID]…` |
| `asset` | `asset` and `cover` (`current`, `previous` or `none`) with an optional `placeholder` (a still picture: an asset id or a path of the folder; required for a new asset) makes that asset the cover of what the media player plays, or of what it played before, shown over the placeholder (`none` makes it a plain picture again), refused when the asset is animated or the value unknown. With `field` (a pointer of the scene such as `/scene/layers/0/image`) and `cover` `current` or `previous`, it shows that cover in the field in one undoable step: the asset `asset` when it already is a cover of that kind, otherwise any cover asset of that kind, otherwise a new one named `asset` (or `asset_2` and so on when the id is taken) over `placeholder`, so that a plain picture never becomes a cover; the picture picker offers it as Cover of what plays and Previous cover (made from the chosen picture, named `cover` and `previous_cover`) and, while a cover is chosen, Shown while nothing plays to pick its placeholder. Otherwise `asset` (a picture of the scene that is not KTX2 yet; an `animated` GIF or WebP becomes an array of its frames with their mean frame time in `KTXanimData`) and `ktx2: true`, optional `codec` (`uastc`, sharper and the default, or `etc1s`, smaller) and `quality` (1 to 100, 80 by default; kept in the file under the key `reiwandQuality` so that tools that rewrite the picture keep it); encodes the picture as a KTX2 texture with Basis Universal and its mipmaps (left out when the asset sets `mipmaps: false`, wrapped when it repeats or mirrors, linear when it is `linear`) at its exact size (a side that is not a multiple of 4 pixels is padded inside the last blocks, and the card draws only the picture), writes `assets/<name>.ktx2` (`<name>-2.ktx2` when another file takes the name) and points the asset at it; undo points it back, and `prune` removes the old file once nothing names it. The assets panel offers it as Convert to KTX2 on each picture, with the codec, a quality slider, the file size before and after and, for each class of graphics card (BC7 on desktops, ASTC on phones, ETC2 on older phones, uncompressed on the rest), the memory it takes there and a crop of the middle of the picture as that card draws it, next to the same crop of the picture now (the graphics card of the app draws the crops of the classes it reads, the processor decodes the others, and each crop says which; `classes[i].drawn` is `gpu`, `cpu` or `plain`); the dialog encodes again on a worker thread a moment after the slider stops | `asset DIR ID [--cover current\|previous\|none] [--placeholder PICTURE] [--field POINTER]`, `asset DIR ID` (the asset, its size and what it takes on each class of card), `asset DIR ID --ktx2 [--codec uastc\|etc1s] [--quality N]`, or with `--dry-run [--looks DIR]` to only report and write the crops. An animated picture becomes an array that keeps the delay of each of its frames; a Radiance `.hdr` or OpenEXR `.exr` picture becomes a UASTC HDR texture that keeps its light above white; `cube: true` makes a cube map of the asset as an equirectangular panorama (faces a quarter of its width), and `faces` (six square picture assets of one size, `+X`, `-X`, `+Y`, `-Y`, `+Z`, `-Z`) makes a cube map of them, written over the asset `asset`; both are refused together, with fewer or more than six faces, with faces of other sizes and for animations. The dialog offers the same shapes with a picker per face, and each class of graphics card that keeps a shape uncompressed (cube maps, arrays whose frames are not whole blocks) says so, its size on the card counted as such; `reiwand-ctl scene asset --ktx2 --cube` or `--faces +X,-X,+Y,-Y,+Z,-Z` does the same |
| `rgb` | `layer` (an adjustment layer) and one or more of `on` (`true` sends what it captures to RGB lighting and sets `permissions.rgb` in the same step, `false` stops and drops the permission when no other layer sends), `size` (`[w, h]`, 1 pixel to twice the canvas: the region around the layer position, which is set to the middle of the canvas when the layer has none) and `whole` (drops `size`: the whole canvas); undo returns both. The inspector of an adjustment layer has a switch for it, Whole canvas and A strip of it, and the strip the lights get, read from the preview 10 times a second through `Rgb1.GetStrip` and drawn as a grid of up to 64 × 16 cells; on the preview the region has corner handles that resize it around the layer position as one gesture, and the layer handles move it | `rgb DIR LAYER [--on true\|false] [--size WxH \| --whole]` |
