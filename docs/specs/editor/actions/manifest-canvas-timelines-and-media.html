manifest one of origin (kind, optional derived_from, provider, source_url; replaces the origin), license (license, an SPDX expression of at most 256 characters), tags (tags, with add or remove instead of replacing them), authors (authors, at most 64; an empty list removes them), credit (paths of files or folders that end in /, license, attribution and an https url; joins the credit that has the same license, attribution and address, up to 256 paths, and refuses a path that is not in the folder), uncredit (paths to take out of the credits; a credit left without paths goes), text (key, language and text, empty or missing to remove: writes strings.<language>.<key>, or the file locale/<language>.json when that language lives in one; a key has up to 64 letters, digits, _, . or - and reiwand. is the engine's) or locale (language and file: moves the texts of a language between the manifest and its locale/<language>.json, and lists or drops locale_files in requires.features) or display (values of fit, zoom, position, background, span, flip and accent, and clear, settings to remove); refuses what the checks of the manifest refuse, each broken field on its own, so an earlier problem does not hide a new one manifest DIR origin KIND [--derived-from AUTHOR|JSON] [--provider NAME --item-url U --credit C [--terms-url U]] [--source-url U], license L, tags T... [--add|--remove], authors A..., display [--fit F] [--zoom Z] [--position X,Y] [--background C] [--span S] [--flip F] [--accent C|prop:ID] [--clear S]
property id, optional min, max and step (sliders only), default and order (its place among the properties of its group, as move_property does); refuses a default outside the range, as validation does property DIR ID [--min N] [--max N] [--step N] [--default V] [--order N]
canvas one of size (size, [width, height] from 1 to 16384 pixels; the Canvas and clock menu of the canvas toolbar offers the screen sizes of the outline field screen_sizes, from HD to 5K, ultrawide 21:9 and 32:9, 16:10, 4:3 and a phone in portrait), clock (optional poster, the second of poster_time, 0 to 86400, rounded to the millisecond, which the same menu takes from the playhead, and seed or fresh, which writes seed: null so that every start picks a new seed; the menu toggles it, a fixed seed of 1 turning it off), bloom (optional threshold, strength, radius, radius_unit (canvas or screen), knee, saturation, limit (a number, or none for no cap), tint, hdr (none to remove the glare above white, or an object with threshold and strength and optionally knee, levels and scatter, merged over the glare it has; a new glare starts from knee 0.5, levels 1 and scatter 0.5; anything else is refused) or off; a new glow starts from threshold 0.8, strength 1, radius 16, canvas, knee 0.5, saturation 1 and no limit, and is written with every field) parallax (optional amount, delay, pointer, gyro or off), camera (optional center, zoom 0.1 to 10, rotation −360 to 360, at and replace, as the transform of a layer writes over keys, or off; the 2D camera of canvas.camera, made when it is missing), camera_grip (hold: move, { "zoom": { "corner" } } or turn, to, a point of the canvas without the camera, optional at and replace: move puts the middle of what the camera shows at to, zoom sets the zoom that puts the corner corner of the screen (fractions) on the line from the middle to to, at its distance, and turn turns the camera so that the top of the view points at to, to the angle nearest to the current one) probe (optional pointer, a fraction of the canvas from 0 to 1 on each side, tilt, −1 to 1 on each side, or off: a simulated pointer and tilt of a phone kept in /scene/ext/editor/probe, which seats::seats feeds to the runtime as a pointer that has settled, so every outline and handle of the canvas shifts by its parallax depth as the scene would) or camera_view (whole: the canvas shows the whole scene with the frame of the camera, kept in /scene/ext/editor/camera2d, which only the preview and the canvas read); the inspector of the canvas shows them with switches and sliders (the glow panel: threshold, strength, radius, soft edge and glow saturation; a switch for the radius in screen pixels; a switch for capping the brightest parts with the cap slider from 0 to 16; a switch for the glare above white with its threshold, strength, soft edge, levels from 1 to 8 and scatter), which change the scene live while dragged, record one step per drag and write nothing when clicked without moving scene act DIR canvas JSON, for example '{"bloom":{"strength":0.6,"hdr":{"threshold":1.5,"strength":0.5,"knee":0.5,"levels":4,"scatter":1}}}' (scene actions lists the fields)
timeline id, optional add (a new timeline, of 5 s unless a length is given; at most 64), remove (every value it drove, keyed or tracked, follows the scene act DIR canvasto (a new id; every value it drives, keyed or tracked, follows), duration (seconds) or frames with fps (1 to 240), wrap (only with repeat), loop (once, repeat or pingpong; leaving repeat drops wrap unless it is given too), speed (−100 to 100), delay (seconds), autoplay, trigger (empty removes it), clear_events (removes every event first), drop_events (names, each removing every event of that name, or name@t only the one at t seconds; refused when none matches) and add_events ({t, name} from 0 s to the end of the timeline, names of 1 to 32 small letters, digits or _, the same name twice at the same time refused, at most 64; kept in order of time); refused when it leaves a problem in the scene. The Timelines menu of the timeline adds, renames and removes them, and Edit the timeline and its events… opens a panel with every field (seconds, or frames at a frame rate; how it loops, wrap while it repeats, a speed slider and field, delay, autoplay and the trigger, named as the inspector names it) and a lane of its events: a double click adds one there, a drag moves one (drop_events and add_events in one step), and the chosen one is renamed or removed timeline DIR ID [--add | --remove] [--rename NEW] [--duration S | --frames N --fps F] [--wrap B] [--loop L] [--speed X] [--delay S] [--autoplay B] [--trigger T] [--clear-events] [--drop-event NAME[@T]]... [--event T:NAME]...
pool id (1 to 32 small letters, digits or _), add (a new mesh pool of 16 shapes of 1024 vertices unless max and max_vertices are given; at most 16 pools), remove (refused while a layer draws the pool, and it names them), max (1 to 256 shapes), max_vertices (3 to 65536), texture (an image asset), size ([width, height], 1 to 4096) and clear (texture, size); refused when it leaves a problem in the scene. The Shape pools list of the Assets panel shows each pool with its size and the layers that draw it, adds a shapes layer on it, changes it in a dialog (name, shapes at once, vertices, picture from the image assets and own size) and removes it; {"pools": {}} of rig lists the pools with their limits, the layers that draw them (layers) and the scripts that declare geometry (scripts) scene act DIR pool JSON, scene ask DIR pools
add_layer with kind shapes a video layer that draws a pool: source names the pool, the first one when it is left out; refused until a pool exists. The source of any video layer offers the pools besides the video assets, so the inspector picks either; the Add layer menu lists Shapes from a pool once there is a pool scene act DIR add_layer JSON
uses path (a script of the scene; every script when left out), add and remove (names of the uses of the script, such as geometry, camera, hierarchy or shortcuts); with neither, it declares what the source of the script uses and its uses lack. A script that gets geometry in a scene with no pool also gets a pool shapes. Importing a script (import_script) detects geometry (scene.mesh), hierarchy (setParent, children, world, parent), shortcuts (openShortcut) and camera along with the other parts; a saved script that uses more than it declares (apart from audio, pointer, clicks and particles) is a problem with a one-click fix (the fix of the problem is the action to send), shown in the Problems list as Fix and in the What the scripts declare list of the Assets panel as Declare what it uses; {"uses": {}} of rig lists, for each script, declared, detected and missing scene act DIR uses JSON, scene ask DIR uses
film one of trim (layer, start, optional end; without end the video plays to its end; the part must lie inside the video, whose length is read from its header, and last 0.05 s or more), rate (layer, rate from 0 to 4) or fit (layer; sets size to the real size of the video); the layer is a video layer or an image layer that shows a video scene act DIR film JSON
sprites one of grid (asset, rows, columns, optional margin, spacing, count and order: rows, columns, rows_reversed or columns_reversed; replaces atlas and rects with the rectangles of the grid), rects (every frame), add, change (index, rect), remove, reorder (from, to), play (fps from 0.1 to 120 and frames, from 1 to the number of frames) and clear; each rect is [x, y, width, height] inside the picture, with at most 256 frames scene act DIR sprites JSON
edit_keys picks (list of {pointer, times}: an animated value or a channel of a clip, /scene/layers/N/clips/M/bones/B/rotation and so on, with the seconds of its chosen keys), and one of these edits as a single step: shift (seconds added), scale (greater than 0) with pivot (stretches the times around that second, along with the length of their handles), lift and value (add to or set the part part of the value: 0 for x or red, 1 for y or green…; lift without part adds to all parts, except the alpha of a color; value on lists and colors requires part), value_scale with value_pivot (multiplies those parts around that value or, without value_pivot, around the middle of the chosen values of each part, and scales their handles with them in units of the value; lift, value and value_scale on keys of several values require them to be of the same type and unit, such as two opacities or two positions), remove (removes the chosen keys, leaving at least one), in_handle/out_handle with aligned (a single key: the given handle and, aligned, the other with the same slope and its own length, or a third of the span if it had none; with part on a list or a color the handle is only of that part, in its units, and the other parts keep the curve they had, written as a list of handles per part; without part the handle is valid again for all), link (joins the per-part handles of a key into a single pair for all, with the shape of the part part, the first if missing), straighten (as in keyframe), or paste: keys inside each pick (keys copied as they are) with at, the second where the first of all falls, replacing the keys of those seconds and animating a value that was fixed. Keys that fall before 0 s or less than a millisecond from another, handles that go past their neighbor and values that the scene does not read are rejected, with messages that name the field; the handles that a moved key leaves longer than its span are shortened up to the neighbor scene act DIR edit_keys JSON
