Shapes, text and fonts#

A shape layer shows the handles of its shape on the canvas while it is selected, placed where the renderer draws the layer (under the parents, groups, anchor, origin and parallax) and listed by the query shape_handles (layer, time): each handle has a name, a kind (edge, corner, round, count, depth, end or point), its position in fractions of the box of the layer (u) and on the canvas (at), and gradient has the ends of the gradient on the canvas. A rect has width, height, size (both, with even the proportions are kept) and radius; an ellipse and a ring the same without radius, and the ring band, its thickness; a polygon has size and sides, which is the second vertex: dragging it around the shape gives the number of sides; a star has size, points, the second tip, and inner, the depth of the points; a line has start, end and width, and moving an end leaves the other where it is by moving position; stroke sets the width of the outline of any shape but a line, and gradient_from and gradient_to the ends of the gradient, which are the center and the edge of a radial one. The handles that resize sit a little inside the box so that they do not cover the grips of the layer. layer figure shape (layer, handle, to, optional even, at, replace) writes what the handle does when it is dragged to to, a point of the canvas in pixels; it keeps the limits of the scene (3 to 64 sides, 2 to 64 points, the depth from 0.01 to 1, a radius up to half the smaller side). layer figure stop (layer, stop) edits the stops of the gradient: { "make": { "radial" } } adds one of two stops, clear removes it, { "add": { "at", "color" } } adds a stop (the color of the one that follows when none is given), { "move": { "index", "at" } }, { "color": { "index", "color" } } and { "remove": { "index" } }; stops stay sorted, between 2 and 4. The inspector shows a bar with the gradient: clicking adds a stop, dragging moves it and every stop has a color and a button to remove it.

The box of a text layer is the size the renderer draws (reiwand-type, the typesetter both share): the same fonts, wrapping, line_height, letter_spacing, padding, background padding and the margin of the outline, shadow and blur, so that the handle box of the canvas matches the text. The query text_box (layer, time) gives its size, the lines it takes, the widest line, the corners and the handle at the right edge, and layer figure text_width (layer, to, at, replace) sets wrap.width so that the right edge lands on to while the left edge stays (it moves position); with remove it takes wrap away. A line break typed in the field of the text (a multi-line box, Enter writes a new line) is part of the text and takes a line of the box.

layer figure fonts (layer, optional font, fallback) sets the font (an empty name takes it away) and the list of fallback fonts of a text layer, at most 8; a letter the font lacks is drawn with the first font of the list that has it. The query font_list (layer) gives the system families installed, sorted, the generic ones, the fonts in the package (assets of font files), the most fallback fonts and the chain of the layer, each name with its source: package, system, generic or missing. The font picker lists them all, each written in its own font and with a search field, and the list of fallback fonts is edited in place, reordered and warns about a font that is neither in the package nor installed.

A value of a text that follows the clock or the date has a format. The binding menu of a text offers the time and the date with ready formats and a sample of each (HH:mm, hh:mm a, EEEE d MMMM yyyy…), and that of a number the time of the day, its hour, minute, second, weekday and month. The field of the format shows what it reads now, from the same formatter the scene uses (query clock_sample: pattern, dates, optional wall, the local time in seconds, and lang), and a help with every piece and its sample (query clock_help: tokens and numbers) whose pieces add themselves to the format when clicked. The text field of a text layer (SceneMultiline) wraps at word boundaries; a run of 2 000 characters or more with no space or line break is wrapped anywhere instead, since wrapping it at word boundaries takes Qt seconds and froze the window (half a minute for a pasted megabyte). Ctrl+V (and the other keys of Paste) reads the clipboard before the text is laid out, so the wrap is chosen first; a megabyte is pasted and applied within a couple of seconds. Check: SceneTextPasteCheck.

Hearing, shaping and trying a binding#

The menu of a bound field has Try and shape the response:

  • Watch the levels of the sound (bindings that follow the sound) opens a meter: the bands of the sound the preview hears, as bars, drawn over a strip in which the bands the binding reads (audio as a band, a range or bass, mid, treble, with of, reduce and channel) are outlined, and a bar with the value the binding gives, before and after bounds, exponent, scale, offset, clamp and invert (not smooth). The meter is the component SceneBandMeter.qml (page, source, of), so the band picker places it over its own strip. It reads the levels with the Bands call of the daemon (dbus.md) about ten times a second, only while the window is open: closing it stops the calls and the daemon forgets the reading. The levels are the ones the scene itself would hear, so the scene must declare and hold the sound grant; without it the meter says so instead of drawing, and it says to play something when nothing arrives. Nothing is recorded, kept or sent. The query meter (source, reading as the daemon gives it, of: 16, 32 or 64) returns the folded bands, the marked range, the value, its raw value and the levels bass, mid, treble, level and beat; with gain: "fixed" in the scene it reads the levels measured from full scale.
  • Try the pointer (bindings that follow the pointer) starts a made-up pointer that wanders over the preview, the same SimulateTouch as Test the pointer and clicks, until it is turned off, which reports the pointer left; the made-up path is the query wander (time).
  • Smooth the changes gives the binding a smooth of 0.2 s and opens it. Every binding of a number, a pair of numbers or a color has the smooth field as a control of its own: one slider for the same time up and down, or two with Different speed going up and down, and a plot of the response of the smoothing to a step up and then down, with the seconds it takes to settle (three times the constant). The query response (smooth: a number or { "up", "down" }, 0 to 60 s) returns the points of the plot.

Next to the menus for scripts (Follow a script lists every script and function of the scene, with its settings) and the media player (above), these complete the entries of the binding menu.

Sound layers#

When the canvas or a sound layer is selected, the inspector lists the sounds of the scene (query sounds): each sound layer with its sounds, its trigger (start when it has none; the picker of play_on offers the buttons and properties of the package, event:, click:, enter:, leave: and media:, see above), whether it is muted, a Listen button and, below, the waveform of each of its sounds. Sound assets that no layer plays have a waveform and a Listen button too. The waveform is the query wave (asset, buckets up to 2048): the peaks of the decoded sound from 0 to 1 and its length, decoded once off the window thread and kept in the cache folder (reiwand/waves, keyed by file, size, date and buckets, at most 256 files), so it is read from disk the next time. The fades of the layer are drawn over it and their two grips set fade_in and fade_out. Listen adds listen to the look of the preview, the layer id or { "asset" } for a sound without a layer: the preview restarts and plays, once, only that sound (the other sound layers are muted, play_on ignored), through the daemon, so the volume and the mute of the application, the setting that turns the sound of scenes off and the sound of other programs apply as they do to a wallpaper. Listening again, or leaving the panel, goes back to the scene. The format has no trim of start and end for a sound: the fades are what shapes the audible part.
