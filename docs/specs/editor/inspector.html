The inspector is generated from the JSON Schema of scenes (reiwand-ctl scene schema) and of user properties (--property), produced by schemars from the types of reiwand-scene, so a field added to the format appears without changes to the editor. reiwand-ctl scene fields DIR POINTER prints the fields it shows for a pointer, as JSON. Annotations in the schema guide the widgets:

Annotation Meaning
x-unit unit shown after the label (px, °, s)
x-range [min, max] of a slider
x-ref the value names an image, video, sound, font or lut asset, a layer (for the layer shape of a particle emitter only the layers that may feed its system), a particle system, a control point of the system (p0 and the points it has), a timeline, a user property that holds a value or, for the trigger of a timeline, the triggers listed for trigger above, or a built-in effect, offered in a list; properties show their labels and effects their translated names
x-key a keyframe [time, value, ease]
x-curve a list of [t, value] points edited on a plot: a click adds a point, a drag moves it (one gesture) and a double click removes it; t goes from 0 to 1 across and the value up to its x-range, or a quarter above the highest point

Numbers become sliders when they have a range, colors a color picker, enumerations and tagged variants a list with translated names, pairs two fields, x-curve lists a curve, color stops [t, color] rows of a time and a color picker, fixed pairs of points (a box, a line) two rows of coordinates, objects a group of fields and other lists a count with Add and Edit…. A number that may also be a name, such as clock.max_fps (display), has the widget of the number and the names in options, shown as check boxes. The app names every field, value, effect, easing, layer kind, asset kind, language and computed feature in the language of the desktop; a test fails when the schema gains one without a name, and another walks the inspector of every starting point with every layer kind, variant and list item and fails when an option or a shown value has no Spanish translation or a raw JSON field holds a plain text. Effect parameters come from the descriptor of the effect, with its label as title and its help as help (a tooltip) in the language of the desktop; a texture or mask parameter offers the image assets and every other layer, written as { "layer": "<id>" }, and every entry of options is a field of its own, with its values as choices and bindable to a property. A parameter that has a handle on the canvas draws it while the effect use or that field is selected, placed where the renderer puts it under the parents, groups, anchor, origin and parallax of the layer, and the query effect_handles (layer, index, time) lists them with their canvas position, what the descriptor declares being what decides: a position parameter, a vec2 named center, source or pivot, or the four corners of a homography effect such as perspective (linked as a quad) is a point; a vec2 from −1 to 1, such as a direction, is an arrow from the first point of the effect (the middle of the layer without one) with a handle at its tip; a float with unit: degrees, such as the angle of spin or waves, is a dial: a ring around that same point with a handle that turns it, and the field gets a dial next to its slider, as particle emitter angles already have; two points whose names pair up (from and to, start and end, a and b, first and second) are joined by a line that has a handle in the middle to move both, and Shift while dragging an end moves both; every drag is one gesture of effects grip. A package effect gets all of this from its own descriptor. An adjustment layer with size shows what it captures: the rectangle (in the coordinates of its parents, around its position as origin places it, without its own turn, skew or scale, as the renderer captures it), a dashed frame with the most it may capture (twice the canvas on each side, in red when size goes past it), with redraw the outline where the capture is drawn again, a caption with the size and the limit, and eight handles on the corners and the middles of the sides that edit size, not the scale (the generic scale handles are not drawn for such a layer). The query capture_view (layer, time) lists them with their canvas positions and is null for any other layer. Above the fields of an effect use, the inspector offers its mask: none, a picture, another layer or a shape (rect, ellipse, ring, polygon, star or line, each started at half the size of the layer), the channel, invert, a soft edge (a slider up to 200 px and a field up to 10000) and the strength, all through effects mask; the same panel offers the mask of a layer itself (layer.mask, which takes the same fields; effects mask without index), with a thumbnail of what the mask looks like (the channel of the picture, the outline of the shape, the name of the masking layer). The canvas draws the mask while its use, the layer or the mask is selected, placed where the renderer draws the layer under its parents, groups, origin and parallax: the box of the layer in dashes; for a picture, the channel it reads (brightness, red, green, blue or alpha), inverted and weakened as the mask is, as a tinted picture over the layer, decoded to at most 512 pixels and kept as a PNG in the cache of the app (the 32 most recent kept); the masking layer framed; and for a shape, its outline filled where the effect applies (everything but the shape when invert is on), its feather range as two dashed outlines, one grown and one shrunk by feather, and handles: the corner that sizes it (the end of a line, which runs from −to/2 to to/2 around the middle), a dot past its edge that sets the feather, and one more for the width of a ring, the corner radius of a rect and the depth of the points of a star. The query mask_view (layer, optional index, time) lists all of it in canvas pixels, mask_picture (asset, channel, invert, strength, tint) returns the file of the tinted channel, and the action effects mask_grip (layer, optional index, hold: size, feather, width, radius or inner, to: a point of the canvas, optional at) writes what a handle does when it is dragged to to, in the pixels of the layer around its middle: size doubles the distance (each side of a rect, ellipse or ring, the larger of the two for a polygon or a star, to of a line), feather is the distance past the edge (across the line for a line), width and radius are measured inwards from the edge, and inner is the distance of the inner points over the outer radius; it is refused for a handle the shape does not have. Each drag is one gesture. The outline adds effect_names, the name of each effect of the package in the language of the desktop, and effect_catalog, the id, category, description and cost of every built-in effect and effect of the package, the description in the language of the desktop. Add an effect opens a dialog of that list grouped by category, searchable, each effect with its cost next to its description; reiwand-ctl scene catalog DIR [--category CAT] [--json] prints the same list on a terminal, as a table or one JSON object per effect. Problems and refused actions show the message of the validator and of the editor, the same English text that reiwand-ctl pack check and the commands print, under a translated heading; each problem leads to its field, whose name is translated. A field that may follow a source (keyframes, a user property, the sound analysis, the pointer or the media player) has a menu to add or remove a keyframe at the current time, bind it, make it fixed again or edit the binding itself (timeline, loop, scale, offset, clamp, smoothing). Follow the media player lists only the media values that fit the field (the texts of the track for a text, the numbers and playing for a number, playing for a switch, the colors of the cover for a color), each with what it would show now with the simulated player of the Player tab; on a text that follows the track, Text while nothing plays opens its placeholder with a row per language. An optional field that is set can go back to its default.

The texture parameters that are gradients (gradient) show a gradient bar under the picker: click it to add a stop, drag a stop to move it, click its swatch to recolor it, remove it with its button; the bar draws the ramp live, and when the editing pauses or ends it runs ramp once (one undo step), making the picture again when the parameter already names a ramp made here. A lut parameter shows the same bar and makes a color table. The bar is the component SceneStops.qml, shared by every gradient of the editor: its stops property takes a list of [position, "#rrggbb"] (positions 0 to 1, at most 8) and its committed(stops) signal gives the new list once per finished edit, never while a stop is being dragged; the owner decides what to write (the ramp action here, the gradient start of the particles, the stops of a shape gradient). The initial colors of the particles (pair, hues, palette or gradient) have their pickers in SceneSwarmTint.qml, with the hue strip, the palette swatches with their noise sliders and SceneGradientBar.qml, through particles ... start. A package effect declares what it needs only through its parameters: a position for a point on the canvas (the canvas handles are driven by the descriptor), and texture parameters named gradient, map, phase, walls, reflection or depth to get the tools above; the picker of every texture parameter offers the assets, white, black, noise, normal_flat and the other layers. The Player tab is a simulated media player for the preview, so that a scene can be made without music playing: a switch turns it on, and it has the title, artist and album of a made-up track, whether it plays, its position and length (sliders up to four hours, with a field in seconds for longer tracks, and times written as m:ss or, from one hour, h:mm:ss as the scene writes them) and its cover, picked among the pictures of the scene, with the five colors that the scene would get from it (or from the placeholder) as swatches. Next track gives the made-up track another title and the next picture as its cover, so that what waits for media:track and media:cover starts in the preview, and the playing switch starts what waits for media:play or media:pause; the track and its cover stay the same while only the position, length or playing change. Scripts of the preview get the made-up track too, with their media events, and reiwand-ctl media simulate OUTPUT FILE.json shows one on any screen (dbus.md, SimulateMedia). The preview then shows that track instead of what a media player plays, without asking for the media grant and without reading the players; it reaches only the preview.

A web package has the same simulated player in its Player tab (switch, title, artist, album, a cover picked among the pictures of the folder, next track, playing, position and length), which feeds window.reiwand.onMedia of the page in its Preview tab: the track goes in preview.media of the PreviewScene document (dbus.md), so the page gets it, with the cover as a blob and the colors of the cover, without the media grant and also when the page loads after the track started; it reaches only the preview. Under it, On a screen still shows a made-up track from a JSON file (Simulate from a file…) on the screen the package was last built and applied to (Stop ends it), the same SimulateMedia call reiwand-ctl media simulate makes; until the package is applied once, the tab says so instead. reiwand-ctl scene preview DIR --media FILE.json shows the track in the preview of a page from a terminal.

The Try tab of the scene editor (and of a page package) shows the user properties of the manifest with the same widget as the settings of a shown package, followed by the presets of the package. Choosing a preset or changing a value sets values and preset in the look of the editor, and the preview document carries the merged map in preview.values (PreviewScene in dbus.md): the daemon shows the package with those values, over the defaults of its properties, instead of the values saved for the package, and never writes them anywhere. Secret texts and buttons are left out. Back to the defaults ends the trial. reiwand-ctl scene preview DIR --values FILE.json [--preset ID] does the same from a terminal; the file is a JSON object of property ids and values and --preset names a preset of the manifest whose values the file then overrides. rig with {"tried": {...}} returns the form (fields, presets, values) that the tab draws.

Test the pointer and clicks, a toggle of the canvas toolbar, turns the mouse over the preview into a made-up pointer on it instead of an editing tool: while it is on, moving or pressing over the preview calls SimulateTouch (dbus.md) with the position as fractions of the output and the held buttons as a bitmask (1 left, 2 right, 4 middle), so input.cursor, click:, enter: and leave: can be tried without a real pointer there; turning it off, or the pointer leaving the preview, reports it left. reiwand-ctl input touch OUTPUT X,Y [--buttons N] does the same on any screen, real or preview:, and --stop ends it.
